# B4.1 Owner Decision Worksheet

**Status:** Owner approved on 2026-10-26 for the recommended defaults below. The approval authorizes synthetic, paper-only B4.1 implementation; it does not authorize provider access, live data, deployment, or trading.

**Source of truth:** The contract proposal is [B4.1-PAPER-PORTFOLIO-VIRTUAL-BROKER-CONTRACT.md](B4.1-PAPER-PORTFOLIO-VIRTUAL-BROKER-CONTRACT.md). This worksheet is the source of truth for the approved B4.1 choices. The B3.2 values are observed reference behavior; only the explicitly labeled recommended defaults became B4.1 policy.

## How to answer

The owner approved the recommended default for each item. The inventory, loss, drawdown, and enforcement-window fields that the recommendation intentionally left owner-specified remain required versioned configuration; implementation fails closed when they are absent.

## 1. Portfolio identity and initial wallet

**Owner must choose:** What makes a `portfolio_id` stable and unique: one portfolio for the local store, one per strategy, one per strategy plus dataset/fixture, or another partition? Choose the genesis wallet in `USDT` and `BTC`, including whether initial BTC is allowed and its cost basis.

**Existing B3.2 reference:** A `VirtualPaperVenue` instance owns one in-memory state. Its test defaults are `10,000 USDT`, `0 BTC`, and no entry price. Explicit initial BTC is supported only with an explicit positive average entry price.

**Recommended default (proposal):** Use one stable portfolio per strategy, instrument, and selected synthetic fixture lineage; require an explicit immutable genesis record; start with `10,000 USDT`, `0 BTC`, and no initial inventory. Permit initial BTC only when the owner supplies quantity and cost basis in the genesis configuration.

**Alternatives and consequences:**

- One portfolio per replay/run improves isolation but prevents continuity across runs and makes portfolio history harder to inspect.
- One shared portfolio across strategies enables aggregation but requires ownership, conflict, and serialization rules from item 7.
- Nonzero initial BTC makes sell and drawdown scenarios available immediately but changes cost basis and PnL comparability.
- A different wallet changes every balance, sizing, risk, and evidence expectation; it must be versioned with the portfolio identity.

**Blocks in B4:** genesis creation, portfolio lookup, replay lineage, initial accounting, and any order that depends on available cash or inventory.

## 2. Costs, prices, precision, and minimums

**Owner must choose:** Fee and spread units, slippage application, mark-price source, price and quantity precision, rounding mode, quantity step, and minimum notional. Decide whether off-step inputs are rejected or rounded, and where rounding occurs.

**Existing B3.2 reference:** Fee is `10 bps` per side, spread diagnostic is `1 bps` per side, slippage is `0.5 bps` per side, and inventory is marked at event `last`. Buys use `ask`, sells use `bid`, and adverse slippage changes fill price. B3.2 uses positive floating-point values and has no quantity step, precision, or minimum-notional rule.

**Recommended default (proposal):** Preserve those B3.2 cost and mark formulas as versioned configuration; use fixed decimal accounting with `0.01 USDT` price/cash precision and a `0.000001 BTC` quantity step; reject off-step submitted values; apply half-even rounding only at ledger boundaries; require a `10 USDT` minimum notional; report spread and slippage separately without double deduction.

**Alternatives and consequences:**

- Keep arbitrary positive floats and no minimum preserves B3.2 most literally but permits irreproducible dust and hidden binary-rounding differences.
- Venue-style precision and rounding may resemble an exchange but adds policy surface and can change fills, fees, and PnL at boundaries.
- Round submitted values instead of rejecting them is convenient but silently changes an order; rejection is easier to audit.
- A different mark (`mid`, bid, or ask) changes equity, unrealized PnL, drawdown, and limit outcomes even when fills are unchanged.

**Blocks in B4:** order validation, fill-price calculation, fee/accounting math, equity and PnL, minimum-order rejection, and deterministic replay fingerprints.

## 3. Matching, fills, queue, cancellation, and expiry

**Owner must choose:** Market and limit trigger rules; full or partial fills; queue priority; whether a later order can leapfrog an earlier one; cancellation command semantics; and whether orders expire, by event time or synthetic time.

**Existing B3.2 reference:** Market buys fill at `ask` and sells at `bid`; limit buys require `ask <= limit_price` and limit sells require `bid >= limit_price`. Orders are fully filled, processed in submission order, and remain open when their condition is not met. There is no cancellation or expiry.

**Recommended default (proposal):** Keep full fills; use deterministic accepted-order FIFO, with replay ordering `(submitted_at_ms, order_id)`; preserve the B3.2 market/limit triggers; add an explicit idempotent cancel command; leave expiry disabled until an owner selects a synthetic event-time TTL.

**Alternatives and consequences:**

- Partial fills require available-liquidity, residual-quantity, fee, and cancellation rules and expand the state machine substantially.
- Price-time or pro-rata priority changes which order receives a fill and requires a defined tie-breaker.
- Event-time expiry is replayable; wall-clock expiry is not deterministic and cannot affect matching.
- Implicit cancellation on replacement or restart risks losing an order unless each transition is recorded.

**Blocks in B4:** order state transitions, fill allocation, open-order snapshots, cancel/expiry commands, and matching acceptance cases.

## 4. Numeric order, exposure, cash, loss, and drawdown limits

**Owner must choose:** Per-order notional, maximum BTC inventory or gross exposure, cash floor, loss window and threshold, drawdown high-water definition and threshold, and whether limits apply at submission, reservation, fill, or all three.

**Existing B3.2 reference:** `max_order_notional_usdt` defaults to `1,000 USDT`; cash and BTC cannot become negative; sells cannot exceed current BTC. B3.2 has no inventory cap, loss window, or drawdown policy.

**Recommended default (proposal):** Retain the `1,000 USDT` per-order cap and nonnegative cash/inventory checks; require explicit owner values for inventory, loss, and drawdown before implementation; fail closed when any configured value or measurement window is missing. Measure loss and drawdown from marked equity using the approved item 2 mark and a versioned event-time window.

**Alternatives and consequences:**

- A larger cap or disabled inventory limit increases scenario coverage but expands loss and recovery impact.
- Daily loss and rolling drawdown answer different questions; choosing one alone leaves the other unbounded.
- Submission-only checks can be bypassed by multiple open orders; reservation-time checks reduce that risk but need shared-state accounting.
- Fill-only checks are simpler but can accept a set of orders whose combined exposure cannot be honored.

**Blocks in B4:** deterministic risk evaluation, order acceptance/rejection, reservations, exposure reporting, and loss/drawdown evidence.

## 5. Approval identity, scope, expiry, revocation, and recovery

**Owner must choose:** Whether every mutating paper command requires approval or only exceptions; the exact approver identity or identity set; whether approval covers one command, an order class, or a session; expiry basis and duration; revocation rules; and recovery behavior for pending or used approvals.

**Existing B3.2 reference:** The venue has no approval layer. B4.1 requires approval to be explicit whenever policy demands it, distinct from deterministic risk approval, and never inferred from a missing record.

**Recommended default (proposal):** Require a named owner approval for each order command; bind it to `portfolio_id`, command type, and exact payload fingerprint; make it single-use; expire it by a pinned synthetic event-time deadline; allow revocation before use; and recover pending approvals as pending, never approved.

**Alternatives and consequences:**

- Session or class approvals reduce operator work but widen blast radius and complicate revocation.
- Multiple approvers improve separation of duties but add quorum and recovery rules.
- Wall-clock expiry is familiar but breaks deterministic replay; event-time expiry is replayable.
- Auto-recovery of pending approval is convenient but can create an unreviewed command after a crash.

**Blocks in B4:** command acceptance, approval-pending/rejected states, idempotent retries, recovery, and audit inspection.

## 6. Ledger, checkpoints, atomicity, retention, backup, and migration

**Owner must choose:** Ledger format and location; hash-chain and checkpoint contents; commit atomicity guarantee; checkpoint cadence; retention and deletion authority; backup destination and restore test; and schema migration/version-compatibility policy.

**Existing B3.2 reference:** State is in-memory and fingerprints are deterministic. It does not provide a durable ledger, checkpoint, crash recovery, backup, or migration policy.

**Recommended default (proposal):** Store an append-only UTF-8 JSONL ledger under the selected portfolio store root, with immutable genesis, sequence continuity, prior/result fingerprints, and a per-event hash chain. Checkpoint after every committed event as a derived accelerator; commit ledger records atomically before exposing the new snapshot; retain the full ledger indefinitely; keep backup opt-in and local; migrate by writing a new schema version with an explicit tool, never by rewriting history.

**Alternatives and consequences:**

- SQLite or another database improves transactional grouping but adds a storage dependency and migration surface.
- Periodic checkpoints reduce writes but increase replay time and crash-recovery work.
- Retention limits save space but weaken audit and replay history unless archival is defined.
- In-place schema rewrites are simpler operationally but destroy the evidence needed to explain old fingerprints.

**Blocks in B4:** persistence format, crash recovery, replay equivalence, tamper detection, retention jobs, backup/restore, and schema compatibility.

## 7. Portfolio sharing and concurrency

**Owner must choose:** Whether a portfolio may be used by multiple strategies, datasets, or processes; who owns shared commands; how commands are serialized; whether reservations are portfolio-wide; and what happens on a concurrent idempotency or version conflict.

**Existing B3.2 reference:** Each venue instance has one mutable order list and position. It has no cross-strategy sharing or process concurrency contract.

**Recommended default (proposal):** Keep portfolios isolated per strategy and fixture lineage as in item 1; permit one writer at a time per portfolio; serialize accepted commands under an exclusive local lock and a monotonic ledger sequence; reject stale snapshot versions and conflicting idempotency payloads.

**Alternatives and consequences:**

- Shared portfolios support aggregation but require explicit membership, fair ordering, reservations, and conflict reporting.
- Optimistic concurrency allows more readers/writers but needs version retries and deterministic conflict outcomes.
- A process-local lock is small and safe for one local worker; multi-process or network locks require ownership and recovery semantics.

**Blocks in B4:** shared portfolio creation, multi-strategy order flow, reservation accounting, command ordering, and concurrency recovery.

## 8. Read-only CLI/API inspection resources

**Owner must choose:** Exact resource names, identifiers, filters, pagination, and response schemas for local read-only inspection; whether CLI and API expose the same resources; and the fixed store-root and access boundary.

**Existing B3.2/B3.13 reference:** B3.2 exposes snapshots in Python; B3.13 inspects synthetic run/accounting artifacts. Neither defines B4 portfolio resources or mutation routes.

**Recommended default (proposal):** Expose the same versioned JSON resources in CLI and GET-only local API: portfolio summary, snapshot, open and terminal orders, fills, ledger events, risk outcomes, approval outcomes, fingerprints, and replay/recovery status. Require explicit `portfolio_id` and validated store root; support bounded filters and pagination; keep inspection free of replay, strategy execution, and mutation.

**Alternatives and consequences:**

- A smaller surface is easier to maintain but makes audit and recovery diagnosis harder.
- Raw ledger access is useful for forensics but needs stable schema and pagination limits.
- Rich server-side aggregation improves convenience but risks duplicating accounting or risk logic outside the domain layer.
- Any POST or command route expands the approval and idempotency boundary and must be separately approved.

**Blocks in B4:** resource schemas, CLI/API parity, read-only acceptance tests, audit workflows, and recovery inspection.
