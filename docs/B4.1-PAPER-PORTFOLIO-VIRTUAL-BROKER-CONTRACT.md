# B4.1 Paper Portfolio and Virtual Broker Contract

**Status:** Owner-approved implementation contract on 2026-10-26. It defines an offline implementation boundary; it does not approve provider access, live market data, deployment, or trading.

**Purpose:** Freeze the smallest shared BTCUSDT Spot portfolio and virtual broker contract before B4 implementation. B4 consumes synthetic/fixture events and produces paper-only state and fills. B3 E1/E2 and B3.1–B3.13 remain unchanged.

## 1. Scope and ownership

- Instrument: `BTCUSDT` Spot only, long-only, with `USDT` quote cash and `BTC` inventory.
- Inputs: explicitly synthetic or recorded fixture market events with stable IDs, timestamps, and fingerprints. Events are processed in strictly increasing event time; no wall clock or provider transport is needed for replay.
- Outputs: immutable paper portfolio events, orders, fills, snapshots, accounting, and replay evidence. These are simulations, not venue orders or claims about real fills.
- Market OS owns input validation, risk, sizing, portfolio state, persistence, recovery, and paper execution. Jev remains advisory-only and cannot approve, size, or execute an order.
- Inspection through CLI/API is read-only. Any command-submission interface is a separate local paper-only operation, governed by this contract's validation, idempotency, approval, and risk gates.

This contract continues the boundaries in [PHASES.md](PHASES.md), [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md), [ADR-0019](decisions/ADR-0019-u3-b3-jev-experimental-boundary.md), and [JEV_PROCESSING.md](../JEV_PROCESSING.md). The existing [B3.2 virtual venue](../market_os/paper_venue.py) and [B3.3 synthetic replay](../market_os/synthetic_replay.py) are reference behavior, not permission to change or promote B3 artifacts.

## 2. Contract identities and immutable state

Proposed internal Market OS schema identities (not exchange or provider wire schemas):

| Artifact | Proposed identity | Semantics |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio contract | `market-os.b4-1.paper-portfolio@v1` | Portfolio identity, policy identity, and replay lineage |
| Portfolio event | `market-os.b4-1.paper-portfolio-event@v1` | One immutable accepted command, approval, market event, order transition, fill, or recovery record |
| Order | `market-os.b4-1.virtual-order@v1` | A validated paper order and its lifecycle state |
| Fill | `market-os.b4-1.virtual-fill@v1` | An immutable simulated execution derived from an eligible synthetic event |
| Snapshot | `market-os.b4-1.portfolio-snapshot@v1` | Deterministically reduced balances, open orders, fills, risk state, and cursor |

The portfolio is the aggregate root. `portfolio_id` is stable and unique within its local store; its immutable genesis record binds the symbol, assets, initial wallet, policy/config versions, and synthetic-only classification. A snapshot is derived from the genesis record plus the ordered event ledger; it is never independently edited. A portfolio ID cannot be reused for a different genesis identity.

Every command, approval decision, market event reference, state transition, fill, and snapshot carries a schema/version and a deterministic fingerprint. State is immutable: new facts append events and yield a new snapshot fingerprint. Wall-clock timestamps may be recorded for operator audit, but cannot affect matching, event ordering, or deterministic replay.

## 3. Portfolio and accounting state

The minimum snapshot contains:

- Portfolio, instrument, quote/base asset, policy/config, and source-fixture identities.
- `cash_usdt >= 0`, `btc_quantity >= 0`, average BTC cost basis, current mark price, and event cursor.
- Open and terminal order records, immutable fills, approval/risk outcomes, and idempotency outcomes.
- Cumulative fees, turnover, spread cost, slippage cost, realized PnL, unrealized PnL, equity, and the snapshot fingerprint.

The B4.1 default accounting proposal follows B3.2: mark inventory at the current synthetic event's `last`; buy against `ask`, sell against `bid`; apply configured per-side slippage adversely; calculate fee from fill notional; include buy fees in acquired cost basis and subtract sell fees from realized proceeds. Unrealized PnL is `(mark_price - average_entry_price) * btc_quantity`; equity is `cash_usdt + mark_price * btc_quantity`. Spread and slippage are separately reported cost diagnostics; they must not be deducted twice if already reflected in fill price. Initial inventory, if allowed, requires explicit quantity and cost basis.

These formulas describe simulated accounting only. They do not imply market-realistic execution. Numeric fee, spread, slippage, initial wallet, precision/rounding, and mark-price settings remain versioned configuration, not hidden defaults.

## 4. Order lifecycle and matching

An order records stable `order_id`, portfolio ID, client command/idempotency key, `BTCUSDT`, side (`buy` or `sell`), type (`market` or `limit`), positive quantity, optional positive limit price, submission event time, config/policy identity, and fingerprint. The initial contract remains long-only: sells cannot exceed available BTC; no negative cash or inventory is permitted.

Proposed order states and transitions:

```text
received -> risk_rejected
received -> approval_pending -> approval_rejected
received -> accepted -> open
approval_pending -> accepted -> open
open -> filled | cancelled | expired
open -> partially_filled -> partially_filled | filled | cancelled | expired
```

Rejected, filled, cancelled, and expired are terminal. An absent approval when policy requires one leaves the command blocked or pending; it never implies approval. Risk approval is a Market OS decision and is distinct from any human approval. No Jev label, probability, ranking, or prose can satisfy either gate.

B3.2 currently supports full fills only and does not model partial fills. B4 must preserve that behavior unless an owner explicitly selects and versions partial-fill semantics. Queue priority, partial fills, cancellation/expiry commands, and market-vs-limit matching conventions must be specified by the selected B4 matching policy; a B4 implementation cannot silently infer them from a real exchange.

## 5. Risk and approval gates

Before order acceptance or fill, Market OS evaluates gates in deterministic order:

1. Validate schema/version, portfolio identity, synthetic source, event freshness/order, and all fingerprints.
2. Resolve idempotency before applying a command a second time.
3. Validate order shape, symbol, balances, and supported order semantics.
4. Apply the pinned risk policy and sizing result against the current portfolio snapshot.
5. Require any approval explicitly demanded by policy; verify approver, scope, policy/config identity, expiry, and single-use semantics.
6. Reserve or consume only funds/inventory allowed by the accepted order and matching policy.

Every risk limit is versioned with an explicit value, unit, scope, and measurement window. The contract must support at least per-order notional, maximum BTC inventory/exposure, cash floor, and loss/drawdown limits; missing or invalid values fail closed. A failure produces a typed `rejected` or `blocked` outcome with stable reason codes and no fill. Limits are applied by Market OS, not by Jev or the dashboard.

Before implementation, owners must decide exact limits, whether approval is required for every order or only exceptions, who may approve, how approvals expire/revoke, and whether an approval authorizes one command or a bounded session. No implicit unlimited policy is valid.

## 6. Idempotency and command behavior

Every mutating paper command has a caller-supplied stable idempotency key, scoped to `portfolio_id` and command type, plus a canonical payload fingerprint.

- Same key and same payload: return the original recorded outcome and resulting fingerprint; append no second fill or balance change.
- Same key and different payload: reject with an idempotency-conflict outcome; leave portfolio state unchanged.
- New key: validate and record once, including rejection/blocked outcomes where needed for deterministic retry behavior.

Order IDs, fill IDs, event IDs, and command IDs are unique within their declared scope. Duplicate submission cannot create duplicate approval, order, fill, or ledger effects. The persistence layer must make command outcome and its ledger event recoverable as one logical operation.

## 7. Persistence, integrity, recovery, replay

Persist an immutable genesis/config record and append-only, ordered event ledger. Each event binds sequence number, event/command identity, prior-state fingerprint, source-event fingerprint when applicable, resulting-state fingerprint, and its own fingerprint. Checkpoints are derived accelerators only; they cannot supersede or rewrite the event history.

Recovery starts from the verified genesis and replays events deterministically. Validate schema versions, sequence continuity, event-time ordering, idempotency uniqueness, source references, fingerprint chain, risk/config identity, and recomputed snapshot. On missing, malformed, unsupported, or tampered data, return an explicit recovery failure and keep mutation disabled. Do not repair, skip, or replace ledger entries silently.

Repeated replay of the same ordered synthetic events, commands, and pinned configs must produce identical order/fill/accounting state and fingerprints. Reset/replay must not consult network, providers, external clocks, or mutable files outside the selected fixture/store root.

## 8. Read-only inspection surfaces

The CLI and versioned local API may list and inspect portfolio summaries, snapshots, orders, fills, ledger events, risk/approval outcomes, fingerprints, and replay/recovery status. Inspection must not mutate state, submit commands, rerun strategy logic, or rewrite artifacts. Responses identify the portfolio, config/policy versions, source fixture, event cursor, and relevant fingerprints.

Store roots and portfolio IDs are validated; path traversal and unsupported resources fail closed. API inspection uses GET-only routes. Mutation methods, if later implemented for local paper commands, must be separately scoped, explicitly named, idempotent, policy-gated, and incapable of reaching a live venue. CLI/API presentation code owns no accounting, risk, sizing, or matching logic.

## 9. Preserved boundaries and non-goals

- B3 E1 remains the existing numerical B2 conditional-rate control; B3 E2 remains numerical input plus one recorded Jev assessment. Their meanings, artifacts, evaluation, and not-promoted status are unchanged.
- B3.1–B3.13 remain unchanged and synthetic/fixture-only. B3.13 CLI inspection is not a portfolio execution surface.
- No Binance transport or exchange account/balance/order access; no Jev, TypeSafe, OpenAI, or other provider request; no credentials; no real market-data acquisition.
- No live trading, leverage, margin, shorting, futures, perpetuals, funding, liquidation, or production deployment.
- No provider adapter, prediction claim, model-owned risk decision, or automatic promotion is part of B4.1.

## 10. Implementation-readiness gates

B4 code may start only after all of the following are satisfied:

1. Owner accepts or revises this contract and its proposed schema identities.
2. B3.13 is closed with exact CLI test evidence recorded; B3 E1/E2 and B3.1–B3.13 are verified unchanged.
3. Owners freeze portfolio identity/genesis semantics, initial wallet, fee/spread/slippage and mark-price configuration, rounding/precision, and matching/order lifecycle behavior.
4. Owners set numeric risk limits and define approval scope, identity, expiry/revocation, and failure behavior.
5. Persistence/recovery format, retention, and compatibility/migration policy are selected.
6. Synthetic acceptance cases cover buys, sells, limit behavior, insufficient funds/inventory, risk rejection, approval pending/rejection, idempotent retry/conflict, fees/cost accounting, crash recovery, replay equivalence, and fingerprint tampering.

Passing these gates permits only the specifically approved local synthetic paper implementation. It does not permit provider calls, real-data acquisition, deployment, shadow study, or live execution.
