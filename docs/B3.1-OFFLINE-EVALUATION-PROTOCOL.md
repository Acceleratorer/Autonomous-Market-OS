# B3.1 Offline Evaluation Protocol

**Status:** Document-only protocol proposal. It records an offline evaluation
method and proposed study choices; it does not authorize implementation,
provider use, data acquisition, or execution.

**Scope:** BTCUSDT Spot, B2-derived five-minute targets, and recorded B3.1
fixtures in local research artifacts.

This protocol is read with the [B3.1 question contract](B3-1-QUESTION-CONTRACT.md),
[JEV_PROCESSING.md](../JEV_PROCESSING.md), and the decision boundary in
[ADR-0022](decisions/ADR-0022-b3-1-decision-freeze.md). It defines how a later
offline study could be evaluated. It does not claim that the proposed arms,
thresholds, or sample rules are implemented.

## 1. Scope and non-goals

### In scope

- Replay of an approved local dataset revision and immutable recorded
  assessment fixtures.
- BTCUSDT Spot, 1-minute closed candles, B2 point-in-time features, and the
  B2 five-minute target.
- Leakage-controlled chronological splits, target joins after outcome
  availability, calibration, fusion, metrics, and failure reporting.
- Deterministic artifacts, fingerprints, coverage counts, and reproducible
  reports.

### Non-goals and hard boundaries

- **Offline only.** The protocol makes no provider, exchange, Binance,
  TypeSafe, Jev, credential, network, or new data-acquisition request.
- It does not activate a provider adapter, infer a provider capability, or
  authorize context egress.
- It does not authorize paper execution, live trading, orders, risk overrides,
  leverage, margin, futures, perpetuals, funding, derivatives, or real capital.
- Synthetic or recorded fixtures are contract and replay evidence. They cannot
  support a predictive-edge, profitability, causality, or deployability claim.
- Existing B3 E1/E2 remain unchanged, offline, recorded-response-only, and
  not promoted. This protocol neither renames nor rewrites them.
- Jev assessment probabilities remain probabilities over Jev's declared
  semantic labels. They are not market-return probabilities.
- This document does not create, require, or imply `approved-policy.json`.

The roles and promotion boundary follow [ADR-0015](decisions/ADR-0015-btc-math-first-jev-experiment.md),
[ADR-0019](decisions/ADR-0019-u3-b3-jev-experimental-boundary.md), and the
[U3/B3 benchmark report](U3-B3-JEV-INCREMENTAL-BENCHMARK-REPORT.md).

## 2. Evaluation artifacts and invariants

Every later run must persist, or reference immutably:

1. the dataset revision and raw-provenance manifest fingerprint;
2. B2 feature and target identities;
3. the canonical feature, label, split, calibration, fusion, and arm
   configuration identities;
4. fold boundaries, purge and embargo decisions, and a fold fingerprint;
5. fixture identity, response fingerprints, and normalized B3.1 status counts;
6. matched-row intersections for every comparison;
7. metrics with sample counts, coverage, uncertainty method, and failure
   reasons; and
8. the final report fingerprint and replay result.

An artifact is eligible for comparison only when its identity, timestamps,
provenance, and replay fingerprint are internally consistent. Missing,
unavailable, stale, malformed, late, or abstaining results remain explicit
records. They are not silently turned into zeros, neutral probabilities, or
successful predictions.

## 3. Dataset eligibility

### 3.1 Approved local revision

The study must name one owner-approved local `dataset_revision` before any
split or model fit. The revision is a content identity, not a mutable folder
name. Its manifest must include the canonical raw-input fingerprint, source
references, row count, symbol, interval, time range, and the code or
normalization revision used to materialize it.

The study may read an existing local artifact store. It must not fetch a newer
revision, repair history from a provider, or merge rows from an unrecorded
source. A revision change starts a new study identity and requires the same
eligibility checks again.

### 3.2 Candle and coverage requirements

An eligible research dataset contains closed, canonical BTCUSDT Spot 1m
candles with:

- one unambiguous row for each included minute;
- strictly increasing minute timestamps and a declared UTC range;
- finite OHLCV values with valid units and deterministic row fingerprints;
- source availability recorded for every row; and
- enough contiguous history for the B2 five-row lookback and the five-minute
  future outcome path.

The research dataset must provide continuous coverage across each evaluated
window. A row is not eligible merely because its current candle exists: every
required lookback candle and every candle in the five-minute target path must
be present and available under the point-in-time rules.

### 3.3 Immutable provenance and replay

The raw manifest and normalized rows are immutable inputs. A replay of the same
revision, normalization identity, and configuration must reproduce the same
canonical bytes, row fingerprints, feature fingerprint, label fingerprint,
split fingerprint, and report fingerprint.

Provenance records must distinguish event/publication time from first-observed,
ingestion, and Market OS availability time. Later corrections, revised text,
or rows first observed after the decision time cannot be used to backfill an
earlier observation.

### 3.4 Leakage and gap rules

- Features may use only values available by `decision_eligible_at_ms`.
- Realized labels, future rows, future returns, fills, account state, and
  target-derived fingerprints stay in the offline label store; they are absent
  from the feature packet and any recorded question input.
- A missing lookback candle makes the feature row ineligible (`gap` or
  `warm_up` according to the B2 status). A missing target-path candle makes
  the label unavailable.
- A stale or unavailable source remains a status with its reason. It is not
  imputed from a later row.
- Duplicate rows are resolved only by the repository's deterministic
  canonicalization rule. Conflicting duplicates fail eligibility when the rule
  cannot establish one canonical row.
- Split-boundary overlap is handled by the purge and embargo rules in Section
  5, even when a fixture is small.

### 3.5 Smoke dataset versus research dataset

| Dataset class | Permitted purpose | Required interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Smoke dataset** | Schema parsing, normalization, replay, split wiring, fixture coverage, and failure-path checks. It may be synthetic or in-memory and may be deliberately small or gappy. | It is not evidence of predictive performance, calibration quality, market behavior, or Jev value. Empty or unavailable metrics are expected. |
| **Research dataset** | Leakage-controlled predictive and calibration evaluation over an approved local revision. It must be real local research data with immutable provenance and continuous coverage in evaluated windows. | It may support claims only for the frozen offline target and evaluation population, with uncertainty, costs, coverage, and all gates reported. |

The current B3 fixtures are smoke or recorded-response artifacts. They do not
become research data by being replayed more times.

### 3.6 Minimum rows and labels

Structural eligibility requires at least one valid, leakage-safe labeled row in
each split that the run claims to measure, plus enough rows to compute every
declared metric. Rows without a realized target are counted and excluded from
target metrics; a run with no eligible labels is **ineligible** for predictive
or calibration claims.

Exact sample and power thresholds are an owner decision. The following is a
**proposal, unapproved** and must not be treated as a frozen gate:

- a smoke run may use any count that exercises the intended branches;
- a research run should target at least 10,000 eligible labeled rows overall,
  at least 1,000 calibration rows, and at least 1,000 final-test rows per
  reported outer fold; and
- a fold or regime with insufficient support is reported as unavailable,
  rather than pooled silently into another fold.

These numbers are planning defaults only. They do not convert a small dataset
into evidence of edge.

## 4. Target contract

### 4.1 Primary B2 target

For a feature row at close time `t`, the primary numerical target is:

```text
target_return_5m = ln(C[t+5] / C[t])
```

The binary label is:

- `positive` when `target_return_5m > 0`;
- `non_positive` when `target_return_5m <= 0`, including an exact zero; and
- unavailable when the exact future path or target candle is missing, stale,
  or not available under the dataset's point-in-time cut.

The target identity remains `btc.b2.close-return-5m.v1`. B2 feature inputs do
not contain the future target row or its target fingerprint. The outcome is
joined to the feature only after the outcome is available and only in the
offline evaluation artifact.

### 4.2 Economic target kept separate

Executable net return is a later economic target. It must use post-decision
prices and explicitly modeled delay, fees, spread, slippage, and any other
approved cost assumptions. It is not interchangeable with the sign of the B2
five-minute log return, and it is not created by relabeling a Jev assessment.

No current B3.1 fixture authorizes an economic target or paper-fill study.

## 5. Chronological evaluation

### 5.1 Frozen ordering

Freeze the dataset revision, feature and target identities, arm definitions,
fixture identity, calibration and fusion methods, metric definitions, cost
assumptions, and all proposed thresholds before reading final-test outcomes.

Rows are sorted by feature close time. Random shuffling is prohibited.

The proposed outer ordering is:

```text
earliest train -> optional validation carved from the training history
               -> calibration -> final held-out test
```

Validation is a model-selection range nested inside the pre-calibration
history; it never uses calibration or test outcomes. For the frozen B3 E1/E2
benchmark, the existing `train -> calibration -> test` path remains unchanged
and no validation range is retrofitted into B3.

For a later rolling or expanding study, each fold has its own ordered train,
validation (if used), calibration, and test ranges. The final test period is
read once after every transform and threshold is frozen.

### 5.2 Purge and five-minute embargo

The target horizon is five minutes. At every split boundary:

1. purge a row when its target end time is at or after the next boundary;
2. embargo the first five minutes after the boundary; and
3. apply the same rule at train/validation, validation/calibration,
   calibration/test, and any rolling-fold boundary.

The B2 implementation expresses this as `target_end_ms >= boundary` for the
purge and a five-minute post-boundary embargo. Rows assigned to `purged` are
retained in split metadata with the reason and never enter a fit or metric.

### 5.3 Fold identity and reproducibility

Each fold records the ordered time bounds, row identities, counts by status,
purged rows, embargoed rows, label counts, regime counts when regimes are
declared, and a canonical fold fingerprint. A rerun with the same inputs must
produce the same fold assignment and fingerprint.

The final report must state the exact matched test intersection for every arm
comparison. A higher-coverage arm may not receive an advantage by being scored
on different test rows.

### 5.4 Proposed minimum fold and regime rules

The following are **proposals, unapproved**:

- use at least three non-overlapping outer test folds, with an untouched final
  period reserved for any later shadow decision;
- require every measured fold to contain both binary labels and the owner-
  approved minimum sample count;
- require declared market regimes to appear in each reported fold when the
  regime analysis is part of the claim; and
- flag a fold as unstable when a regime or status class exceeds an owner-
  approved concentration limit, rather than hiding that concentration in an
  aggregate score.

The regime definition, concentration limit, and minimum fold counts remain
human decisions. A fold that fails an approved rule is reported as unavailable
or unstable; it is not silently merged with another period.

## 6. Experimental arms

### 6.1 Existing B3 meanings preserved

- **E1 numerical B2 conditional-rate control:** the existing numerical B2
  conditional-rate arm over the frozen feature input.
- **E2 numerical inputs plus one recorded Jev assessment:** the existing B3
  same-input arm, using only a usable recorded response on rows where one is
  present. Missing, malformed, late, inconsistent, hostile, unavailable, and
  abstaining responses remain explicit outcomes and reduce E2 coverage.

E1 and E2 retain their current split, calibration, fixture, result, offline,
and non-promotion meanings. This protocol does not change their code or
results.

### 6.2 Future comparison mapping (proposal only)

The following names map a possible future B3.1 study. They are **proposed
comparison labels, not implemented arms**:

| Proposed arm | Inputs and purpose |
|---|---|
| **N0** | Numerical B2 inputs and deterministic quality fields; the future numerical-only control. |
| **J1** | The same point-in-time evidence packet as N0 plus one normalized recorded Jev assessment; tests same-evidence representation value. |
| **C2** | N0 inputs plus separately timestamped context assessed by Jev; tests a future additional-context treatment. |
| **C2-control** | The same context as C2 represented without Jev; separates context value from Jev representation value. |
| **F3** | Calibrated numerical output fused with eligible J1/C2 evidence and explicit quality or coverage fields under a frozen Market OS policy. |

N0 does not replace E1, J1 does not rewrite E2, and C2, C2-control, and F3
are not present in the current fixture-only scaffold. Every future arm must
share observation IDs, targets, chronological boundaries, purge and embargo,
held-out periods, and cost assumptions.

## 7. Calibration

Calibration belongs to Market OS and is target-specific.

### 7.1 Proposed methods

- **Primary proposal:** binary Platt/logistic calibration mapping a numerical
  score or logit to `P(target_return_5m > 0)`.
- **Weak baseline:** one-bin empirical calibration, retained to make the
  existing B3 path comparable and to expose small-sample limitations.
- **Restricted method:** isotonic calibration only after a separately approved
  sample threshold and preregistered rule. It is unavailable by default.

Multiclass calibration of Jev's semantic labels is outside the primary binary
target. A Jev support, agreement, contradiction, abstention, or concentration
distribution may be a candidate input to a later Market OS calibrator, but it
is not already `P(positive)`.

### 7.2 Fit and use rules

- Fit calibration parameters on calibration rows only.
- Use validation, when declared, only for model or hyperparameter choices that
  are frozen before calibration and test.
- Keep final-test labels and scores untouched until the final evaluation pass.
- Persist method, target identity, fit range, row count, parameters, fallback
  status, and calibrator fingerprint.
- If a calibrator cannot be fit because labels, support, or score semantics
  are insufficient, mark calibration unavailable and report why. Do not switch
  methods silently.

## 8. Fusion

### 8.1 Primary proposal and ownership

The primary fusion proposal is a regularized logistic model owned by Market OS.
It may combine already validated, target-appropriate inputs and produce a
target-specific probability for the B2 binary event. Market OS also owns the
downstream signal, sizing, risk, paper, persistence, and recovery policy.

### 8.2 Candidate input families

The future study may evaluate candidate families such as:

- calibrated numerical probabilities or logits from N0/E1;
- B2 feature-derived scores and deterministic quality indicators;
- normalized Jev support and agreement/contradiction dimensions;
- explicit Jev abstention, unavailable, stale, contradiction, and
  concentration diagnostics;
- response or packet freshness and coverage metadata; and
- separately approved timestamped context for C2 and C2-control.

This list identifies input families for study design. It does not freeze a
feature vector, encoding, regularization value, threshold, or interaction.

### 8.3 Missingness and abstention decision

Missingness encoding is unresolved. Candidate policies include explicit
missingness indicators with a masked value, a separate abstention category,
or a row-level no-fusion outcome. The chosen policy must preserve whether a
result was usable, abstained, unavailable, stale, or invalid and must be
frozen before test scoring.

There is no silent neutral substitution. A missing Jev result cannot become
`0.5`, a semantic unknown cannot become a market probability, and a numerical
fallback must be reported as a separate arm and coverage condition.

## 9. Metrics and reporting

Metrics are reported separately by arm, fold, regime when declared, response
status, and matched test intersection. Point estimates without counts and
uncertainty are incomplete.

The metric names below are **proposals**. Acceptance thresholds require owner
approval.

### 9.1 Discrimination

- balanced accuracy or Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) for the binary
  label;
- ROC-AUC or PR-AUC only when class support and the preregistered use justify
  them; and
- confusion matrices, positive/non-positive support, and matched-control
  deltas.

### 9.2 Probabilistic quality

- log loss;
- Brier score;
- score distributions and observed positive rates by fold; and
- uncertainty intervals appropriate to the chronological dependence.

### 9.3 Calibration

- reliability tables or diagrams;
- expected calibration error (ECE) with the declared binning rule;
- calibration slope and intercept when sample support permits; and
- calibration metrics separated from discrimination metrics.

### 9.4 Stability

- fold means, variance, and worst-fold results;
- regime-conditional results and drift indicators;
- status-conditioned coverage and performance; and
- changes after provider, fixture, dataset, or feature-version identity
  changes, each treated as a new treatment.

### 9.5 Coverage and abstention

- total candidate rows and matched rows;
- usable, abstained, unavailable, stale, malformed, late, and inconsistent
  counts;
- coverage by fold and regime;
- selective risk and risk-coverage curves when a selective policy is tested;
  and
- explicit numerical-only coverage when a Jev-dependent arm is unavailable.

### 9.6 Latency and cost assumptions

Offline fixtures have no observed provider latency, usage, or cost. Record
`unknown-not-observed-offline` rather than zero. A later shadow proposal must
freeze the latency, queue, fee, spread, slippage, and cost assumptions before
reading its test or shadow outcomes.

## 10. Stability and failure policy

### Missing or abstaining Jev results

Keep the raw case, normalized status, reason, timestamps, and fingerprint.
Exclude it from Jev-dependent predictions and report the resulting coverage.
The numerical arm may continue only as its own explicitly named arm.

### Contradictory evidence

A contract-valid assessment with disagreement or contradiction is usable with
an explicit disagreement dimension. It is not a parser failure and does not
prove that either claim is correct. Contradictory or mixed rows must be
reported separately in coverage, calibration, and stability tables.

### Concentrated but valid responses

Retain a concentrated distribution as usable and report its concentration.
Concentration is diagnostic; it is not market confidence, a return
probability, a trading edge, or a reason to reject the row by itself.

### Stale or unavailable results

Apply the declared status precedence and preserve the original reason. A stale
packet or response is stale; a missing or late but otherwise fresh recorded
response is unavailable. No stale or unavailable result is silently replaced
with a neutral value or an unrecorded cache.

### Drift and regime instability

Report performance, calibration, coverage, and failure rates by chronological
fold and declared regime. A provider or model-version change, dataset change,
feature change, or question-contract change starts a new treatment. If an
approved stability rule fails, mark the affected claim unstable and stop the
promotion path for that claim until an owner-approved replan exists.

### Insufficient sample power

If the declared minimum sample or power rule is not met, mark the affected
metric or fold unavailable. Do not pool away the deficiency, widen a threshold
after seeing test results, or describe an underpowered result as a negative or
positive edge finding.

No fallback may silently convert missing Jev output into neutral evidence.

## 11. Acceptance and promotion gates

The gates are separate. Passing one does not imply passing the next.

### Gate A: implementation correctness

Require deterministic parsing and normalization, closed-label validation,
timestamp and provenance checks, future-free packets, replay fingerprints,
fixture coverage, and proof that the normal path performs zero provider or
network requests.

### Gate B: offline evaluation eligibility

Require an owner-approved local research revision, immutable raw provenance,
continuous evaluated coverage, leakage-safe labels, frozen chronological
folds, purge and five-minute embargo, explicit fixture identity, and all
required sample rules. A smoke dataset can pass Gate A but cannot pass Gate B
for predictive claims.

### Gate C: predictive evidence

Require held-out comparison against the applicable numerical control on an
identical matched intersection, target-specific calibration evidence,
discrimination and probabilistic-quality metrics, fold and regime stability,
coverage and abstention accounting, uncertainty, and frozen cost assumptions.
The acceptance thresholds are pending owner approval. Synthetic fixtures cannot
pass this gate as evidence of market edge.

### Gate D: prospective shadow approval

Only after Gates A-C and a separate owner decision may a 90-day shadow proposal
be considered. Before that proposal, the evidence package must include:

- a sufficiently long approved continuous dataset and preregistered power;
- frozen folds, regime rules, calibration, fusion, missingness policy, and
  metric thresholds;
- matched out-of-sample results with uncertainty and worst-fold reporting;
- explicit failure, abstention, stale, latency, cost, and coverage evidence;
- deterministic replay and complete lineage; and
- a written human approval covering duration, criteria, monitoring, and stop
  conditions.

This document does not authorize the shadow period itself. It does not
authorize provider use, paper execution, live trading, derivatives, or any
credential or network request.

## 12. Unresolved human decisions

The following decisions require explicit owner approval before a research
evaluation can be treated as a frozen study:

1. **Dataset length and revision:** which local `dataset_revision`, time range,
   and continuous-coverage standard are approved.
2. **Minimum sample and power:** minimum eligible rows, label balance, per-fold
   counts, uncertainty method, and any power calculation.
3. **Regime coverage:** regime definition, minimum representation, concentration
   limit, and whether regime-conditional metrics are a release gate.
4. **Fold schedule:** number of rolling or expanding folds, exact boundaries,
   validation placement, final untouched period, purge, and embargo schedule.
5. **Calibration and fusion thresholds:** primary score thresholds,
   regularization, isotonic eligibility threshold, and any abstention cutoffs.
6. **Missingness policy:** representation and treatment of Jev missing,
   abstaining, stale, unavailable, late, and contradictory outputs.
7. **Metric acceptance thresholds:** required improvements, calibration limits,
   stability limits, confidence or uncertainty rules, and matched-intersection
   minimums.
8. **Latency and cost assumptions:** permitted latency, queue delay, fees,
   spread, slippage, and cost model for any economic evaluation.
9. **Shadow duration and criteria:** whether to propose a 90-day shadow,
   monitoring cadence, success and failure criteria, stop conditions, and human
   review requirements.

Until these choices are approved, the corresponding values are proposals or
unavailable fields, not hidden defaults.
