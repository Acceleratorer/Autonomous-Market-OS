## Corrected evaluation model

The versioned report is now `market-os.quality-benchmark.v2`. Each case has
three separate outcomes:

1. `benchmark_success`: whether the benchmark procedure and its safety,
   lineage, replay, fallback, and rejection checks behaved as expected.
2. `gate_correctness`: whether the validator accepted or rejected according to
   the case oracle. False accepts and false rejects are listed explicitly.
3. `candidate_quality`: whether an applicable candidate produced the expected
   grounded output. The trusted deterministic baseline is labeled
   `trusted-baseline`; invalid or provider-failure cases are not silently
   counted as useful model output.

## Metric semantics

- Citation precision is valid predicted passage citations divided by all
  predicted citations, including invalid citation IDs in the denominator.
- Citation recall is valid predicted passage citations divided by expected
  accepted passages.
- Empty denominators are reported as `null` with applicable/not-applicable
  counts; they are not converted into a perfect score.
- Evidence coverage is supported claims divided by all claims. It is
  informational and does not imply correctness, completeness, truth, or
  investment quality.
- Exact quotes establish textual support only. Citation presence does not
  prove that a paraphrase or inference is entailed.
- Conflicting, stale, synthetic, partial, and incomplete sources retain their
  source labels and are not upgraded by the benchmark.
- Unsupported statements remain explicit. Relabeling text as inference does
  not turn it into an evidence-supported claim; semantic entailment remains
  outside deterministic validation.
- Missing provider usage and cost are `null`/unknown in the benchmark report,
  not zero. Existing `market-os.model-summary.v1` numeric usage fields remain
  backward compatible; the benchmark translates absent test-provider usage at
  its reporting boundary.
- Latency records measured observations and excludes volatile timing from the
  stable fingerprint. It is informational rather than a machine-specific
  promotion threshold.
- Synthesis and report fingerprints include fixture/case inputs, outputs,
  validation, lineage, and metric semantics, but exclude volatile timings.

## Inspection and CLI/API behavior

The existing read-only benchmark inspection remains available:

```text
market-os benchmark inspect <benchmark-id> --resource cases
market-os benchmark inspect <benchmark-id> --resource metrics
market-os benchmark inspect <benchmark-id> --resource failed-cases
market-os benchmark inspect <benchmark-id> --resource thresholds
market-os benchmark inspect <benchmark-id> --resource fingerprint
```

M2.6 adds:

```text
market-os benchmark inspect <benchmark-id> --resource evaluation
market-os benchmark inspect <benchmark-id> --resource candidate-quality-failures
market-os benchmark inspect <benchmark-id> --resource benchmark-failures
```

`failed-cases` in v2 means candidate-quality failures. True benchmark
procedure failures are exposed by `benchmark-failures`. Existing v1 artifacts
remain readable through the legacy fingerprint path and retain their original
`failed-cases` interpretation.

The HTTP equivalents are:

```text
GET /v1/benchmarks/<benchmark-id>/evaluation
GET /v1/benchmarks/<benchmark-id>/candidate-quality-failures
GET /v1/benchmarks/<benchmark-id>/benchmark-failures
```

Inspection verifies the artifact hash and version-appropriate fingerprint,
performs no provider call, and does not mutate trading state.

## Security and privacy review

The gate still treats retrieved and generated text as untrusted. Exact quote,
citation, document, content-hash, and run-lineage checks remain fail-closed.
Output length and secret-like output checks remain active. The benchmark uses
synthetic internal fixture text and does not log credentials. Future model
output must pass the same gate before publication and must not gain authority
over paper-only orders or risk decisions.

Automated deterministic checks cannot prove arbitrary generated statements
true, detect every semantic hallucination, resolve source conflicts, or prove
that an inference is entailed. Those limitations are explicit in the v2
evaluation output.
